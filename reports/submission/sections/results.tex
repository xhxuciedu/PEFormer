\section{Results}
\subsection{A strong prediction benchmark does not define a design benchmark}
The harmonized source corpus contains 318,471 measurements: 297,962 development/training rows and 20,509 rows in the official held-out fold. The training mixture comprises 174,067 PRIDICT-family rows, 65,594 Liu-derived rows and 58,301 Kim-derived rows. The evaluation fold contains 9,175 Liu-derived and 11,334 Kim-derived rows, with no PRIDICT-family rows. Thus, even the source benchmark changes the mixture of studies relative to training.

We distinguish four experimental settings throughout the paper (Table~\ref{tab:populations}). The original benchmark evaluates source-trained predictors; the initially reserved external library tests zero-shot prediction and selection; historical adaptation uses labels from that library and evaluates a retrospective test subset; and later low-label experiments use the already exposed adaptation validation subset as an outer development audit. These are not four independent biological datasets. In particular, the two adaptation settings draw from the same library used for the zero-shot comparison.

On the original prediction benchmark, the frozen heterogeneous \model{} ensemble reached \rhoS{} $=0.9079$, compared with $0.8690$ for released OptiPrime (Figure~\ref{fig:prediction}; Table~\ref{tab:source}). The paired difference was $+0.0389$, with the original 5,000-resample protospacer bootstrap interval $[+0.0286,+0.0498]$. The difference was larger in Kim-derived records than in Liu-derived records. Within 14 sufficiently populated source/cell/editor conditions, the row-weighted average correlation was $0.8111$ versus $0.7605$, a difference of $+0.0506$ with interval $[+0.0345,+0.0670]$. The gain therefore does not disappear when between-condition differences are removed.

The apparent strength of the prediction benchmark nevertheless overstates what it measures about a fixed-edit choice. Canonicalization of the full corpus yielded 51,766 intended alleles and 175,668 allele/context decision groups. The source evaluation fold contains 2,412 groups with at least two distinct candidates, only 85 with at least three, and none with five. Among these, 1,463 groups have nonconstant measured outcomes and support an informative choice. A conventional protospacer grouping instead pools alternatives across conditions and sometimes across desired alleles. We retain within-protospacer correlation as a descriptive statistic, but do not substitute it for the fixed-edit decision endpoint.

On the informative source decisions, the heterogeneous ensemble's top-choice efficiency was $0.13636$ versus $0.12721$ for OptiPrime, a paired gain of $0.00915$ (95\% interval $[0.00633,0.01215]$). Its measured-best-design hit rate was $0.7423$ versus $0.6535$, and regret was $0.00667$ versus $0.01582$. These are valid source-domain selection gains, but mainly for shallow candidate sets (Table~\ref{tab:decision}).

\subsection{Representation and objective changes improve source prediction, with important controls}
The model encodes aligned wild-type/edited nucleotide pairs and a segment-aware pegRNA stream, joins their representations by bidirectional cross-attention, and conditions the readout on experimental metadata (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Development compared self-attention and bidirectional S4D mixing with proportion and cumulative-threshold supervision. A retrospective two-by-two comparison of out-of-fold predictions gave Spearman values of $0.8798$ and $0.8911$ for Transformer/simplex and Transformer/ordinal models, and $0.9011$ and $0.9082$ for S4D/simplex and S4D/ordinal models. The corresponding average objective and mixer contrasts were $+0.0092$ and $+0.0192$. These are not a newly randomized factorial: one cell is an earlier frozen baseline, and the approximately $-0.0043$ interaction does not establish subadditivity.

The final source system uses five model families, each represented by five official-fold checkpoints. Equal-weight rank averaging was selected during development. A shortlist gate favored five members over four by $0.001011$ on the internal gate population, just above the previously specified $0.001$ near-tie threshold. This narrow choice should not be interpreted as evidence that five members are intrinsically optimal. Complementarity was useful only when a member remained sufficiently accurate: a strongly decorrelated pairwise-loss candidate had standalone Spearman $0.8244$ and reduced ensemble performance by $0.0037$. Conversely, simply adding seeds, capacity or subsampling did not reproduce the gain from the stronger objective/mixer families.

The comparator audit identified an additional training difference. Under OptiPrime's released sampling and row-weight behavior, the Kim source contributes approximately 2\% of effective training weight, while accounting for 55.3\% of benchmark evaluation rows. Adopting those weights in our model reduced matched development Spearman by $0.0136$ on average across three folds. On the first fold, uniform weighting improved Kim correlation by $0.0247$ and Liu correlation by $0.0063$, with the latter interval including zero. This intervention demonstrates sensitivity to weighting in our model. It does not causally allocate a fixed fraction of the cross-model margin to weighting, nor prove that the remaining difference is entirely architectural.

Additional checks supported, but did not make confirmatory, the source comparison. Removing 196 held-out rows with exact design/condition twins in training left Spearman $0.9075$ and a comparator margin $+0.0385$. Kendall's tau-b, discrimination of nonzero outcomes and Spearman restricted to positive outcomes also favored \model{} (Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:suprobust}). A histogram gradient-boosting reference reached pooled Spearman $0.7413$. Because its configuration was chosen from eight settings on the benchmark itself, it is an explicitly test-selected descriptive baseline, not an unbiased independent test of engineered features or biological mechanism.

\subsection{External-library top-choice utility reverses the prediction comparison}
We reconstructed the masked edited-sequence representation in the DeepPrime large variant library and removed records sharing the audited protospacer or canonical allele with the source corpus. The resulting panel contains 118,187 measurement rows, corresponding to 118,174 distinct candidates in 30,475 eligible decisions. Unlike the source decision subset, it contains 8,775 groups with at least five candidates and 2,400 with at least eight. Its outcome distribution and PBS/RTT geometry also differ from those of the source benchmark. The initial panel endpoints, grouping and eligibility were declared locally before the original model scoring.

The five-fold source-trained ordinal--S4D ensemble retained a higher row-level correlation than OptiPrime ($0.7091$ versus $0.6931$), but its top-choice utility was lower: $0.03627$ versus $0.03690$, difference $-0.00064$ with 95\% target-site interval $[-0.00089,-0.00038]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:decision}). The informative-group comparison agreed: $0.0448$ versus $0.0456$ over 24,668 groups, difference $-0.00078$ with interval $[-0.00111,-0.00046]$. Keeping all-zero groups in the primary endpoint therefore did not create the reversal.

The external deficit increased in consequential strata. At depth at least eight, the difference was $-0.00249$ with interval $[-0.00416,-0.00083]$. Where the measured oracle-minus-random gain was at least $0.05$, it was $-0.00329$ with interval $[-0.00451,-0.00209]$. These are descriptive, outcome-defined strata rather than prospective subgroup rules. At top-three and top-five budgets, both models approached the measured oracle on the respective depth-eligible populations (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:depth}). Failure to distinguish their top-three outcomes is not evidence of equivalence, and comparing top-one and top-five means without their changing denominators would be misleading.

Transferring the source calibrator increased the external Pearson correlation from $0.5522$ to $0.6017$, versus $0.5854$ for OptiPrime. This does not repair the ordering deficit. Isotonic calibration cannot reverse a strictly ordered pair, but can create ties: here external Spearman changed from $0.709087$ to $0.708868$. We therefore use the original scores for selection and distinguish absolute calibration from decision improvement.

\subsection{Correcting the training groups helps, but pair exposure is not the demonstrated mechanism}
The historical ranking key used raw wild-type/edited sequence windows whose boundaries vary with design geometry. In the audited training subset, canonical grouping increased the available within-group design pairs from 14,277 to 368,307. Replay of the actual sampler showed that the old sampled ranking pairs all had identical RTT lengths. Canonical grouping increased sampled exposure from 15,695 to approximately 62,000 pairs per epoch.

A single-seed repair improved external utility by $0.00184$ relative to its matched control, but subsequent factorial controls changed the explanation. Across three seeds, canonical batching with the ranking loss yielded source utility $0.13453$, while canonical batching with ranking disabled yielded $0.13459$; the original batching/ranking recipe yielded $0.13360$. Increasing the pair budget to sixteen did not improve the result. The tested benefit therefore survives without the pairwise term and is consistent with an effect of batch composition; the data do not establish restored pair exposure as its cause.

An explicit feature branch improved some source comparisons but did not establish an external OptiPrime win. Recovering and freezing the actual training-fold feature standardization removed an evaluation artifact that had made external performance appear stronger. A five-fold, coverage-matched feature/canonical model reached source Spearman $0.9084$ and external Spearman $0.7096$, yet remained below OptiPrime in external informative-group top-choice efficiency by $0.00100$ (interval $[-0.00131,-0.00067]$). Thus, greater fold coverage, slightly higher correlation and explicit scalar geometry did not suffice. These results do not show that geometry is unavailable in the sequence representation, or that a candidate-set architecture is necessary.

\subsection{Full-budget target supervision improves selection, but separate heads do not preserve deployed choices}
We next partitioned external-library locus components into adaptation training, validation and test roles. Full-budget fitting used 19,357 training groups, and checkpoint selection used 5,561 validation groups. The retrospective test subset comprised 5,557 groups and 23,044 candidates. The test role prevented direct checkpoint selection on those rows, but this was a repartition of an already studied library, not a new independent study.

Ordinary native-ordinal adaptation achieved mean test utility $0.04178$ over three optimizer seeds, compared with $0.04020$ for released OptiPrime (Table~\ref{tab:adaptation}). The gain, approximately $0.00158$ or $0.158$ percentage points of editing efficiency, corresponds to a 3.9\% relative increase and about a 15.7\% reduction in measured oracle regret. Each ordinal seed's paired component interval against OptiPrime was positive. Pooled test Spearman was $0.7771$ versus $0.6922$. This establishes a benefit for target-supervised \model{} relative to the unadapted released comparator, not superiority under equal target-label access.

Separate utility and multitask heads did not yield a replicated target advantage over ordinary ordinal adaptation. The three-seed utility-selector-minus-ordinal difference was $-0.000021$, with interval $[-0.000436,+0.000378]$. A pairwise selector reached $0.04205$ in its single historical test seed, but the two later pairwise replications were evaluated on validation and source retention only; they cannot be counted as three-seed test confirmation. Their validation comparison with ordinal adaptation remained unresolved. Different loss scales also complicate architectural interpretation: the multitask selector's mean maximum softmax probability at temperature one was $0.9823$, compared with $0.3409$ for the capacity-matched shared-score control. Equal nominal temperature did not make these objective geometries comparable.

An audit of the score actually used for selection overturned an early source-retention claim (Table~\ref{tab:heads}; Figure~\ref{fig:heads}). For utility specialization, the old prediction head retained source utility $0.13323$, near initialization $0.13350$, while the deployed selector achieved only $0.12607$. For pairwise specialization, these values were $0.13428$ and $0.11460$. Even a frozen encoder preserved the original predictor but not the new selector's choices. Consequently, retaining a prediction head cannot be presented as retaining the deployed system. The historical claim of markedly better retention from specialization is withdrawn.

\subsection{Stronger optimization controls and label acquisitions change the low-label conclusion}
The next study counted inner-validation labels inside each acquisition budget and evaluated the deployed score on both target and source. An initial 34-fit experiment, using one acquisition subset and three optimizer seeds at a fixed 100-update recipe, favored a frozen-backbone source-anchored pairwise selector over ordinary ordinal adaptation at the nominal 1,000-group budget: target difference $+0.001445$ and source difference $+0.003092$, with positive conditional component intervals. The anchored selector did not establish a target advantage over OptiPrime, and its source-retention interval did not clear the working $-0.001$ noninferiority margin. Soft pairwise distillation reduced replay KL but worsened source utility relative to anchoring, demonstrating that agreement in soft probabilities did not ensure retention of decisions.

We tested these findings with 76 additional fits: twenty optimization controls, twelve source-preservation factorial runs and forty-four new replication fits. Four exact control fits were reused to complete a 48-configuration replication design. No new target-test or independent-panel model evaluation was performed. The optimization screen crossed five adaptation arms with two learning rates and two update horizons. Selection by budget-inner utility produced target utility $0.041101$ for the tuned ordinal control and $0.041304$ for anchoring; the difference $+0.000203$ had interval $[-0.000295,+0.000736]$. Thus the larger initial advantage depended on the fixed optimization recipe and could not support architectural superiority over an adequately tuned internal baseline.

The locked replication crossed three label acquisitions with two optimizer seeds at nominal budgets of 200 and 1,000 groups. Actual totals were 200--202 and 1,000--1,002 because locus components were not split to hit an exact integer budget. The six-fit target means at budget 1,000 were $0.041233$ for ordinary ordinal adaptation and $0.041047$ for anchoring, versus $0.041409$ for OptiPrime (Table~\ref{tab:replication}). Neither internal method established superior target utility. Anchoring also lost its earlier average source advantage: anchored-minus-ordinal source utility was $-0.000217$, with interval $[-0.002714,+0.002329]$.

Acquisition-specific results explain why optimizer replication alone was insufficient. The anchored model's source means were $0.133997$, $0.117906$ and $0.133483$ across the three acquisitions. A frozen backbone and an initially zero residual did not prevent a new readout from making harmful source choices. The target run standard deviation was also larger for anchoring ($0.000356$) than for ordinal adaptation ($0.000131$). These are three acquisitions from one library, not estimates of variability across independent studies.

\subsection{Balanced teacher-margin preservation improves retention at a target cost}
The preservation factorial crossed three objectives---soft pairwise KL, teacher-winner margin preservation and measured expected regret---with source-mixture versus equal-study replay and two penalty weights. All arms used the same informative source-group pool. A balanced teacher-margin penalty of weight one was the only recipe to pass the predefined constrained-inner promotion gate. Its teacher was our own frozen source model; no OptiPrime scores, features or parameters entered training.

Under target-only checkpoint selection, this margin model reached six-fit mean source utility $0.133284$, compared with $0.128462$ for unregularized anchoring. The difference was $+0.004822$ with interval $[+0.003636,+0.006004]$, while the target difference was $+0.000079$ with interval $[-0.000102,+0.000266]$. This supports the tested preservation recipe, but not an isolated causal claim about margin geometry: balanced margin and balanced KL had different source-to-target gradient ratios, and balanced sampling changed the source-study mixture.

With source-constrained checkpoint selection, the margin procedure achieved target/source utility $0.040652/0.133858$ at budget 1,000 (Figure~\ref{fig:replication}). Relative to initialization, target utility increased by $0.001025$ (interval $[0.000716,0.001350]$), and source change was $+0.000223$ (interval $[-0.000821,+0.001351]$). The latter clears the working $-0.001$ margin for this averaged, conditional, exposed-audit comparison. It is not independent or uniform source-domain noninferiority. The procedure fell back to initialization in one of six runs, and all constrained ordinal, fresh-head and unregularized anchored controls fell back in all six runs.

Preservation had an explicit target cost: constrained margin was below target-selected ordinal adaptation by $0.000580$ (interval $[-0.000883,-0.000264]$) and below OptiPrime by $0.000757$ (interval $[-0.001324,-0.000219]$). At budget 200, even its conditional source-retention interval crossed the working margin. Higher target correlation did not eliminate this trade-off: at budget 1,000, target-selected ordinal and margin models had pooled Spearman $0.7320$ and $0.7121$, versus $0.6960$ for OptiPrime, without establishing better top-choice outcomes (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:secondary}).

\subsection{Data and deployment audits delimit the current generalization claim}
A final RNA/DNA-normalized overlap audit found one outer-validation component connected to source records by a shared 19-base spacer core. It comprised one group and eight candidates; none of the connected source rows entered informative replay. Excluding the entire component left the qualitative screened and replicated conclusions unchanged. Historical primary populations were retained, with the stronger exclusion reported as a sensitivity analysis.

The legacy tokenizer maps RNA U to the token named N. On the observed clean A/C/G/U alphabet this is injective and does not discard nucleotide identity, but DNA-formatted new inputs would not be deployment-equivalent without a compatibility adapter or consistently retrained encoding. Saved anchored and margin models preserved all tested decisions across inference batch sizes, with score discrepancies below $2.4\times10^{-6}$. Single-backbone inference timings were approximately $0.753$--$0.761$ ms per pretokenized, GPU-resident candidate on an RTX 2080 Ti; these measurements exclude model loading, preprocessing and transfers and provide no matched speed comparison with OptiPrime.

Independent public-data feasibility remained limited. Corrected ePRIDICT reconstruction yielded 140 K562 designs over 111 alleles but only fifteen decisions with two or three candidates; the 54 HEK293T designs each targeted a different allele. OPED supplements and run metadata did not provide a verified design/barcode/condition linkage sufficient for outcome reconstruction. These panels were not scored by our models. Accordingly, the present evidence includes an initially reserved within-study library transfer test and retrospective adaptation studies, but no completed independent-study confirmation of the final adapted method.
