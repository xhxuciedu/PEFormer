\begin{abstract}
Ranking candidate prime-editing guide RNAs (pegRNAs) is central to selecting effective designs for a desired edit. We introduce \model{}, an edit-aware sequence-modeling framework that combines aligned wild-type/edited representations, a segment-aware pegRNA encoder, cross-attention and experimental-context conditioning. Cumulative-threshold supervision provides an order-sensitive learning signal, complemented by proportion-based models in the source ensemble. On a 20,509-measurement held-out benchmark, \model{} outperformed released OptiPrime in pooled Spearman correlation (0.9079 versus 0.8690), within-condition correlation (0.8111 versus 0.7605), within-protospacer correlation (0.6356 versus 0.5472) and Kendall's tau-b (0.7478 versus 0.6931). The ordinal--state-space ensemble also retained a ranking advantage on an initially reserved external library (Spearman 0.7091 versus 0.6931). These ranking gains were accompanied by improved source-domain selection: across 1,463 informative fixed-edit decisions, the mean measured efficiency of the top-ranked design was 0.13636 versus 0.12721, a 7.2\% relative increase. Target-library fine-tuning further enabled \model{} to outperform unadapted OptiPrime in external selection, reaching mean top-choice efficiency 0.04178 versus 0.04020 across three optimizer seeds on a 5,557-group test subset, a 3.9\% relative increase; each seed's paired 95\% interval favored \model{}. This external selection advantage required target supervision: zero-shot selection did not outperform OptiPrime, and the adaptation test was retrospective within the same library rather than independent-study confirmation. Together, these computational results establish \model{} as a stronger pegRNA-ranking method on the evaluated benchmarks and demonstrate improved selection efficiency in the source domain and after target-library adaptation.
\end{abstract}
