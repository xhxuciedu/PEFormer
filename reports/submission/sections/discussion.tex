\section{Discussion}
Our results support three distinct conclusions. First, \model{} improves source-benchmark prediction and shallow fixed-edit selection relative to released OptiPrime under the recorded source protocol. Second, this advantage does not automatically transfer to selecting the best candidate in a deeper external library: higher pooled correlation coexists with lower achieved top-choice efficiency. Third, target supervision can improve the latter task, but source-preserving adaptation introduces a measurable trade-off and requires stronger controls than optimizer-seed replication alone.

The distinction between prediction and choice is not an argument against regression. If the conditional expected efficiency of every feasible candidate were known, choosing its maximum would solve the expected-efficiency decision problem. Rather, the empirical objectives, sampling, representations and validation populations used to approximate that expectation can emphasize variation that is irrelevant to a fixed-edit choice. Our external results demonstrate the mismatch in a particular setting. They do not establish a universal failure of correlation, a general advantage of selection-specific losses, or a requirement for joint candidate-set scoring.

Several apparently persuasive mechanistic accounts did not survive their controls. Canonical grouping exposed more cross-geometry pairs, but its gain remained when the pairwise term was disabled. Separate prediction and selection heads appeared to preserve source performance until the audit followed the score actually deployed. A frozen residual anchor initially appeared favorable, but a stronger ordinal baseline and additional label acquisitions removed that advantage. Reduced pairwise KL did not guarantee retained choices. These revisions make the evidence narrower, but more actionable: a proposed mechanism must improve the relevant decision over controls matching batching, optimization, initialization, loss scale and deployment head, not merely produce a plausible diagnostic.

The teacher-margin result is a constructive outcome of that process. Explicitly protecting the frozen teacher's top-choice margins, together with source-study balancing, stabilized source outcomes relative to unconstrained anchoring in the tested acquisitions. It did not provide a free improvement. Source-constrained selection reduced target utility relative to the tuned ordinal baseline and OptiPrime. Moreover, different replay objectives produced different gradient magnitudes and interacted with sampling. The current evidence supports the combined recipe as a source-aware candidate, not margin preservation as a uniquely identified causal mechanism or universally superior method.

The source benchmark also should not be read as a contest between learned representation and biological mechanism in general. The compared systems differ in objectives, row weights, model families and ensemble construction. A weighting intervention in one model does not decompose the full between-system difference, and a test-selected tree reference cannot identify the necessity of mechanistic structure. OptiPrime's published applications extend beyond the exact populations evaluated here \citep{hsu2026}. Our claims concern the reproduced released predictor on the stated rows and groups, not those broader applications.

Several limitations are consequential. The study developed adaptively over repeated evaluations, including later audits of the source benchmark and extensive reuse of the external library. Even the full-budget adaptation test is retrospective within that already explored library. OptiPrime did not receive the target labels used to adapt our model, so its defeat there is an engineering comparison against a released tool, not an equal-label comparison of adaptation methods. The source selection audit is shallow and Kim-derived, whereas source-retention validation is dominated by PRIDICT-family groups; neither validates preservation across every pretrained context. Six-fit intervals condition on three acquisitions from one library and do not establish stability across studies. Point-estimate source filters are not confidence guarantees. Finally, measured candidate maxima and efficiency gaps contain assay noise and should not be equated with true biological optima or known per-design uncertainty.

Data and deployment constraints also limit immediate release claims. The exact harmonized corpus includes author-supplied material whose redistribution has not been established. Sequence normalization, edited-window reconstruction and compatibility with the legacy RNA vocabulary require care. The historical population-rank ensemble is not inherently a query-independent production scorer. Our single-backbone timing results exclude preprocessing and do not establish a speed advantage over OptiPrime. Audits and hashed artifacts help detect implementation drift, but cannot substitute for independent linked outcomes or a genuinely excluded-domain pretrained model.

The appropriate next confirmation is computational and hypothesis-driven: freeze a small number of adequately tuned procedures, evaluate the same deployed score on a verified independent set of fixed-edit decisions, and account for all adaptation and validation labels. A study-level holdout reconstructed by excluding that study and connected sequences from pretraining would answer a different, also useful question. Candidate-set models, segment-specific pooling, genuinely separate representation branches and higher-label curves remain options, not completed negative results. They should not be presented as necessary repairs before simpler controls have been exhausted.

In summary, the strongest present contribution is a decision-aligned experimental framework accompanied by a strong source predictor, a demonstrated target-supervision benefit, and a replicated retention trade-off. The final low-label adapted method has not established an external OptiPrime selection win or independent-study generalization. Reporting that boundary is essential to making the computational evidence useful for future guide-design methods.
